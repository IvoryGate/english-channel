# Series A — Daily Talk Style Guide

Human-feel dialogue rules, hook templates, and 起承转合 structure for Ethan & Nora (B1–B2).

Reference: [`competitor_script_analysis.md`](../../../docs/shows/competitor_script_analysis.md) Section A.

## A.1 Situation-first opening (choose; never use verbatim every episode)

```
[Music sting]
Ethan: Hey, hey, English learners. Welcome back to Daily Talk, your cozy place
       to learn easy English through real life conversations.
Nora:  I'm Nora.
Ethan: And I'm Ethan. And this is the English Listening Room. [+ hook-fusing line]
```

Rules:
- Begin with a recognisable situation or failed moment before any welcome line. The scene and stake land within 20 seconds.
- Put the show/brand name once between 30 and 75 seconds; do not make it the first spoken idea.
- Do not make the Word Tour, CEFR callout, comment request, or CTA part of the cold open.
- **Nora intros first**, Ethan second (mirror Class's Anna-first pattern; Nora is the peer-coach lead).
- Wait — per bible, Ethan is the learner and Nora the coach. Class pattern is teacher-first. So **Nora first** (coach), **Ethan second** (learner). Corrected:
- Dual intro lives **inside the welcome sentence** (word ~20–60), never a separate beat.
- "English Listening Room" spoken once right after dual intro — this is the brand beat.
- Hook fusing line: a question, teaser, or thesis that launches the episode.

## A.2 Hook templates (pick one per episode)

| ID | Pattern | Template | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Slice-of-life → universal feeling | "You know that feeling on Sunday night when… you just don't want Monday to come?" | Warm, low-stakes |
| B | Confessional mini-story → playful reaction | "Nora, I have to tell you something. I almost cancelled today's recording." / "Oh no. What happened?" | Character-driven comedy |
| C | Contrarian / paradox thesis | "Today we're talking about a kind of strength that does not need to be loud." | Philosophical mindset |
| D | Parallel teaser + comic anecdote | "This can totally change your life if you get it right. / And ruin your day if you get it wrong. / Like that time I shouted at my coffee machine." | Behavior topic |
| E | Interactive game / question | "Okay, before we say anything deep, let's play a tiny game. Rate your week 1 to 10 — but you can't pick seven." | Year-review / comeback |

## A.3 Human-feel dialogue rules

### Rhythm
1. **Strict alternation as default** (Ethan→Nora→Ethan→Nora).
2. **Multi-line runs allowed**: same host may take 2–3 consecutive turns for a brief story or teaching beat. Max 4 sentences in a run.
3. **Short reactive beats as handoffs**: `Yeah.` / `Right?` / `Exactly.` / `Mhm.` / `Oh, I love that.` — connective tissue, not content.
4. **Long/short sentence variation**: short (≤8 words) and medium (12–20 words) alternate. Avoid 3+ long sentences in a row.

### Fillers (human feel)
- **Allowed sparingly**: `uh`, `um`, `you know`, `I mean` — max 1 per 8–12 turns. Prefer on Ethan (learner) turns.
- **Backchannels allowed freely**: `Mhm`, `Hmm`, `Oh`, `Oof`, `Ouch`, `Wow`, `Ooh`, `Aw`.
- **Laughter beats**: `[laughter]` tag ~6–10× per episode.
- **Forbidden**: `like` as filler (allowed as verb/quotative only).

### Inline vocab gloss (signature teaching move)
When a strong word first appears, the *other* host asks for meaning:
```
Nora:  I was relentless that week.
Ethan: Relentless — that means you didn't stop, right?
Nora:  Yes. I kept going, again and again.
```
Happens ~8–10× per episode. This is what makes it "learning" not "chat".

### Emotion / delivery cues
Every turn carries `[Delivery: …]` per DELIVERY.md. Cues are natural-language performance phrases, not enums:
- Good: `warm encouragement rising`, `hesitant learner realizing`, `quiet conviction`
- Avoid: `dramatic`, `shouting`, `excited`

## A.4 起承转合 structure (A series: 30/40/20/10)

| Phase | Word position (of ~1800–2400) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 起 (30%) | 0–30% | Cold-open sting → welcome + show name → dual intro → hook → topic announcement → B1/B2 callout → CTA + comment prompt → "who is this episode for" |
| 承 (40%) | 30–70% | 3–5 teaching ideas, each: metaphor + personal anecdote (Ethan or Nora) + inline vocab gloss. Strict alternation with occasional 2-line runs. |
| 转 (20%) | 70–90% | **Word Tour** — slow vocab recap of ~8–10 words. Format: `Word. Meaning. Two examples. "Repeat: ___."` |
| 合 (10%) | 90–100% | Recap of 3–5 ideas in one breath → optional homework → comment prompt → CTA → sign-off: `This is Nora. / And this is Ethan. / And you've been listening to Daily Talk, from the English Listening Room. Bye.` |

## A.5 Series A differentiation (vs Class)

- **More natural filler tolerance** — Class forbids `uh`/`um`; we permit sparse use on Ethan for human feel.
- **Per-turn `deliveryCue`** — audiobook parity for emotional specificity (Class has none).
- **Ethan/Nora dynamic** — peer relationship, not teacher-student. Ethan asks the "dumb" question without shame; Nora answers as a friend who knows slightly more, not as a teacher.
- **Brand name spoken** — "English Listening Room" once per episode (Class doesn't say their channel name mid-episode).

## A.6 Title formula

`[Specific daily situation or tension] | B1 English Conversation`

Examples:
- `Someone asks “Tell me about yourself” — what do you say? | B1 English`
- `The conversation goes quiet — how do you keep talking? | B1 English`
