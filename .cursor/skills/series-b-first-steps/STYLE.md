# Series B — First Steps Style Guide

Human-feel dialogue rules, contrarian hooks, and 起承转合 structure for Riley & Sam (A2–B1).

Reference: [`competitor_script_analysis.md`](../../../docs/shows/competitor_script_analysis.md) Section B.

## B.1 Frozen cold-open chassis (use verbatim every episode)

```
[Music sting]
Riley: Hi, I'm Riley.
Sam:   And I'm Sam. Welcome to the English Listening Room. [+ hook teaser line]
```

Rules:
- **Riley intros first** (coach), **Sam second** (learner) — mirror J&May's May-first pattern.
- Intro is **fused with hook teaser** — no separate "welcome" sentence.
- "English Listening Room" spoken once right after dual intro — brand beat.
- Hook teaser: a one-line contrarian claim, scene, or embarrassment setup.

## B.2 Hook templates (pick one per episode)

| ID | Pattern | Template | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contrarian-number (default, most viral) | "Today: only 5 tenses. Not 12. Five." | Rule-breaking / simplification |
| 2 | Mundane-scene | "You're at the coffee shop. The barista asks your name. You freeze." | Daily-situation topics |
| 3 | Embarrassment-story | "I once said 'I'm fine' at a funeral. I wanted to disappear." | Social-anxiety topics |

Contrarian-number template structure:
```
Sam:   Today: only [N]. Not [expected N]. [N].
Riley: Sounds wrong, right? But by the end, you'll use [topic] like a native.
```

## B.3 Human-feel dialogue rules

### Rhythm — learner-asks / teacher-explains / learner-mirrors / teacher-praises

Default 4-beat loop, repeated across the episode:

```
Sam:   [learner question — short, 1 sentence, often with a filler]
Riley: [teacher explanation — 2-4 sentences, gloss every new word]
Sam:   [learner mirror — repeat the key phrase, apply to self]
Riley: [teacher praise — "Exactly." / "You got it." / "Nice."]
```

Example:

```
[Delivery: hesitant learner, searching]
Sam:   So wait... "used to" is only for the past?

[Delivery: patient clear explanation]
Riley: Right. "Used to" locks the habit in the past. It means: not anymore.
       "I used to study French" — so you don't now.

[Delivery: relieved understanding]
Sam:   Not anymore. Okay. So "I used to study French" means I don't now.

[Delivery: warm praise]
Riley: Exactly. You got it.
```

### Rhythm variations
- **Strict alternation** as default (Sam→Riley→Sam→Riley).
- **Riley may take 2-line runs** for longer explanations. Sam stays short.
- **Sam may take 2-line runs** only for a brief personal anecdote (max 3 sentences).

### Fillers (human feel)
- **Sam (learner) — moderate**: `uh`, `emmm`, `hmm`, `you know` allowed 1 per 4–6 Sam turns. Real A2 learners hesitate.
- **Riley (coach) — near-zero**: no `uh`/`um`. Only `Okay.` / `Right.` / `Mhm` as backchannels.
- **Forbidden**: `like` as filler (allowed as verb only).

### Inline vocab gloss (signature move)
Riley glosses **every** new word immediately, in the same turn:

```
Riley: "Relentless" — that means she didn't stop. She kept going.
```

Gloss ~6–8 words per episode. This is what makes A2-B1 feel "learning" not "chat".

### Long/short sentence variation
- Average sentence: 8–12 words.
- Short reactions (1–3 words): `Oh.` / `Wait.` / `Really?` / `Hmm.` — used as handoffs.
- Riley's teaching turns: 2–4 sentences, each 8–12 words.
- Sam's turns: 1–2 sentences, often shorter (5–10 words).

### Emotion / delivery cues
Every turn carries `[Delivery: …]` per DELIVERY.md. Cues are natural-language:
- Good: `hesitant learner, searching`, `patient clear explanation`, `relieved understanding`, `warm praise`, `surprised recognition`
- Avoid: `dramatic`, `excited`, `shouting`

## B.4 起承转合 structure (B series: 25/45/20/10)

| Phase | Word position (of ~1400–1900) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 起 (25%) | 0–25% | Brand sting → dual intro → contrarian-number hook → "By the end you will [X]" contract → A2/B1 callout |
| 承 (45%) | 25–70% | 3 teaching beats, each: learner question → teacher explanation (with gloss) → learner mirror → teacher praise. Short sentences. |
| 转 (20%) | 70–90% | **Meta pivot** — mindset / identity / role-play. "But here's the thing most learners miss..." Shift from rules to real-world confidence. |
| 合 (10%) | 90–100% | One-sentence recap → **one-sentence comment challenge** → CTA → sign-off: `This is Riley. / And this is Sam. / And you've been listening to First Steps, from the English Listening Room. Bye.` |

## B.5 Series B differentiation (vs J&May)

- **Sam gets more personality** — J&May's Jay is a flat learner-proxy; our Sam has doubts, jokes, mild resistance. He's a real friend, not a prop.
- **More filler tolerance on Sam** — J&May's Jay is too clean; real A2 learners hesitate. We allow `uh`/`emmm`/`hmm` on Sam.
- **Per-turn `deliveryCue`** — audiobook parity (J&May has none).
- **Brand name spoken** — "English Listening Room" once per episode (J&May doesn't say their show name mid-episode).

## B.6 Title formula

`Learn [N] English [topic] — [contrarian claim] | Easy English Podcast A2-B1`

Examples:
- `Learn 5 English Tenses — Not 12, Just 5 | Easy English Podcast A2-B1`
- `Learn English Small Talk — 3 Lines, Not 30 | Easy English Podcast A2-B1`
