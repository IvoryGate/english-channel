## Competing Product Hypotheses

English learning is a candidate, not a constraint inherited from the repository
name. Research these alternatives before choosing where to spend production
time. The alternatives are not simultaneous new series.

| ID | Audience problem and product promise | Reusable advantage | Main uncertainty and rejection condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | A2-B1 adult learners: understand a real-life conversation and say one useful next line | Existing scripts, two-host adapters, learning catalogue and visual identities | Reject the current treatment if listeners cannot follow it or existing viewers show no repeated interest after quality improves |
| H2 | General adult listeners: a short original story about a difficult everyday choice, with a satisfying consequence | Narration, dialogue and illustration tools; no English-teaching wrapper required | Story quality and performance may be harder than teaching; reject if it needs costly animation or clinical-sounding advice to be interesting |
| H3 | Mandarin-speaking adults: clear, short explanations of everyday work and communication problems, with original examples | The operator can assess language and specificity directly; shared publishing/rendering infrastructure | No verified audience/channel fit or accepted Mandarin voice here; reject if free audio cannot pass listening review or research finds no distinct audience need |

H1 is the lowest-cost prototype because its assets already exist. That is a
cost observation, not a market victory. Do not impose US women aged 25-44 as
the actual audience merely because older Shorts manifests contain that target.
Choose geography, language and viewer need explicitly for each hypothesis.

Primary references checked on 2026-11-06 establish product mechanisms, not
current view or subscriber benchmarks:

- [Easy English](https://www.easyenglish.video/) combines everyday spoken
  English, subtitles and supporting practice. Its
  [podcast](https://www.easyenglish.video/podcast) uses recurring hosts,
  listener questions and interactive transcripts. Study continuity from
  watching to participation, not its paid membership as an immediate product.
- [High Level Listening's programme list](https://www.highlevellistening.com/videos/)
  groups understandable everyday situations such as commuting and errands.
  This is a topic-clarity reference for H1, not a measured performance cohort.
- [TED-Ed](https://ed.ted.com/about) pairs original animated explanations with
  questions and resources. Its expert production model is an adjacent quality
  reference for H2/H3, not an affordable production template for this machine.
- [The School of Life](https://www.theschooloflife.com/about-us/) connects
  work, relationships and self-understanding across films and other products.
  This verifies an adjacent positioning, not demand for our treatment or
  authority to offer psychological treatment. Original everyday fiction can
  explore choices without diagnosing viewers.

There is no freshly verified direct Mandarin competitor cohort in this slice.
H3 therefore remains incomplete, not a low numerical score disguised as fact.

## Research Protocol And Decision

Use existing free access, local tools and included model allowances. Do not
start paid APIs, trials requiring payment, subscriptions or ads. If a source
is unavailable, record the missing field and continue the independent work;
do not substitute guessed analytics or circumvent access controls.

1. Capture the channel's available last 90 days and previous comparable period:
   video ID, format, language/level, duration, publication time, topic, traffic
   source, impressions, CTR, views, watch time, retention and subscribers gained.
   Save export timestamp, window, source and raw file hash. Keep unavailable
   values null; never infer private retention from public views.
2. For each hypothesis, first inspect six recent comparable videos from at
   least three creators, plus two evergreen examples. Include ordinary recent
   uploads, not only winners. Separate Shorts from long-form and comparable
   age windows. Record URL, capture time, publish time, title, duration,
   visible views, opening promise, scene changes, participation and distinct
   value. Public views/day is descriptive and age-biased, not growth velocity.
3. For a promising hypothesis, compare each candidate with the median of up to
   ten recent same-format uploads from its own creator in a comparable age
   band. Record unavailable samples. Review a small sample of public comments
   for expressed needs, paraphrasing without copying handles or personal data.
   Creator size, topic, distribution and age remain confounders.
4. Use Google Trends with fixed region, language, date window and YouTube
   Search filter where available; preserve the exact comparison and capture.
   Scores are normalized sampled interest, not absolute volume or a poll.
   Do not rank differently normalized queries as if they share one scale.
   [Google's data explanation](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en)
5. Produce one decision page with strongest evidence, counterevidence, unknowns,
   required production minutes and the next falsifiable test. Agent research
   budget: one bounded collection pass and one focused gap-filling pass per
   week. Reuse unchanged evidence; refresh trends after seven days and direct
   competitor captures after 28 days before a new topic lock.

Score each hypothesis on 0-4 with evidence IDs: audience need (25%), distinctive
viewer benefit (20%), attainable audio/story quality (20%), production and
review cost (20%), and repeat-visit/community mechanism (15%). For each axis,
0 means evidence against, 1 weak support, 2 mixed support, 3 repeated relevant
support, and 4 support from both external evidence and an owned-channel test.
Missing evidence is null, not 0. Weighted score = sum(weight * score / 4),
only when all axes are observed; report coverage and confidence separately.

No score authorizes publication. A local prototype needs a clear viewer need,
an original treatment, a feasible zero-new-spend route and a named quality
test. A public pilot also needs actual audio acceptance, usable media,
publication policy and a measurement window. A material language/format/channel
change is presented as a concrete pilot decision at the weekly owner review.
An H2/H3 pilot on the existing audience cannot establish a new market's fit;
mark audience mismatch or propose a separate distribution test for review.

## One Creative Contract

The sample [`creative-brief-v1.json`](../configs/quality/creative-brief-v1.json)
is a complete planning example, not a JSON Schema, executable pipeline config,
finished script or approved release. No renderer consumes it in this slice.
The sample makes a small H1 prototype reviewable without choosing H1 as the
channel's final direction.

Every future brief should carry audience/hypothesis, one viewer outcome,
opening payoff, story beats, performance intent, two packaging concepts,
reference hashes, safe zones, prompt versions, rights/source notes and review
results. Preserve actual generator/model revisions and hashes after generation;
null provenance prevents claiming the asset is ready. Store outputs in the
canonical content workspace, not in this tracked planning JSON.

Existing visual rules conflict: `VISUAL_IDENTITY.md` and parts of
`thumbnail_templates.md` describe PIL lettering, while the latter's production
workflow and `host_visuals.py` use generated lettering. October checkpoint
`docs/shows/BODY_VISUAL_STYLE.md` and `docs/shorts/VISUAL_STYLE.md` contain newer
owner-approved body treatments, but are not merged on this branch. Do not let
this experimental brief silently override either production path. The next
visual integration must select one active version, label superseded rules and
test the actual adapter. No existing published media needs a cosmetic rebuild.

For this local pilot, generate no-text art, then use existing deterministic
lettering to compare two thumbnail concepts with the same title and exact
headline. This is a proposed experiment setting, not a global default change.
Keep identity, palette and lettering consistent; vary the visible proposition:
a two-person urgent situation versus a close-up of the speaker making a calm,
limited offer. A wardrobe change alone is not a useful experiment.

Validate the composition at about 320x180, including readable headline,
recognizable action, identity, anatomy and thumbnail/opening agreement. Average
brightness and file dimensions cannot validate emotional meaning. Body art
serves readable captions and continuity; it need not recreate every sentence.
Start with one approved still per story location and bounded motion. Measure
render time before expanding scene count. Never invent word alignment or
claim a generated still is an actual audience member or real event.

YouTube distinguishes reusable series identity from interchangeable substance;
the substance must deliver distinct value. New clothing or wallpaper is not
the educational or creative variation the business needs.
[Official monetization guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

## Four-Week Test And Community Loop

Weeks are relative to baseline completion and pilot acceptance, not promises
that all four weeks of evidence exist today. These are maximum test volumes,
not replacements for current programming or permission to restart automation.

| Week | Agent work | Owner review and exit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Reconcile current evidence; compare H1/H2/H3; prepare a 60-90 second audio/story sample for the best supported feasible hypothesis | Blind listening and one concrete pilot decision; no automatic public rebrand |
| 2 | Produce at most two accepted pilot episodes; capture production cost; run a thumbnail-only native test on one eligible long video with useful exposure | Check one assembled sample and the exception list; no extra releases to fill a quota |
| 3 | Produce at most two matched follow-ups with one named content improvement; derive at most one useful Short per episode; incorporate one real viewer question if available | Review comments and retention evidence; identify topic/traffic confounding |
| 4 | Compare eligible T+7 cohorts and completed native tests; review actual cost, audio preference and participation; schedule later T+28 observations in the existing work queue | Continue, revise, stop or collect more evidence; expansion requires repeated support |

Retain current published content. No paid traffic, duplicate-upload A/B tests,
unrequested comments or new recurring automation are part of this slice.
Native YouTube tests support up to three title/thumbnail combinations and use
watch time; Shorts and private videos are not eligible. Changing a title or
thumbnail during a native test ends that test. Prefer one well-exposed eligible
video, freeze its title for a thumbnail-only test, and accept an inconclusive
result instead of manufacturing a winner.
[Current official A/B rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)

For cross-episode content comparisons, label results observational. Compare
format, duration, topic, traffic source and publication age; changes in audio,
story and packaging together do not isolate the cause. Capture T+24h, T+7 and
T+28 where available. Retention takes time to process and highlighted moments
may be unavailable for small videos; absence is not zero retention.
[Official retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)

The weekly scorecard has five rows: packaging (native test and watch minutes
per 1,000 eligible impressions), content (30-second retention and practice-beat
drop-off), relationship (returning audience and substantive participation),
quality (blind listening and content errors), and cost (elapsed production,
review minutes and rerender rate). For the impressions metric, use watch time
attributable to those impressions; do not divide all-source watch time by
thumbnail impressions. Report numerator, denominator, missing data and sample
size. Regular-viewer status requires long-term return behavior, so it cannot
be a four-week success gate.
[Audience definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13615784?hl=en)

Community closes the learning loop: an episode asks one answerable question;
a later post or reply explains the answer; recurring misunderstandings enter
the next brief with evidence IDs; the next episode visibly addresses that
need. For the sample: ask for a helpful sentence with a time limit, then use a
new situation in the follow-up so viewers must transfer the phrase. Do not
invent comments when none arrive. Use an explicitly channel-authored example.
YouTube recommends posts that complement uploads and actual interaction.
[Official post guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11912631?hl=en)

Before any separately authorized community execution, reconcile historical
post/comment IDs and existing channel replies to prevent duplicates. Keep
draft, attempted, scheduled, verified-public and blocked separate; store exact
copy, content ID, remote ID/URL and verification time in the existing local
ledger. Pending replies older than the service target are visible exceptions,
not a claim that an unattended agent replied. This document does not reactivate
the paused community task or authorize sending messages.

Budget the owner's weekly review at 45 minutes: 10 minutes evidence and
exceptions, 15 minutes blind audio/assembled sample, 10 minutes creative and
audience feedback, 10 minutes decisions. A 30-minute week uses one sample;
never expand the slate to compensate. Agent reports only a decision, changed
evidence or a real blocker. Subscriber ambitions remain long-term outcomes;
the first win is a repeatable product viewers choose to return to.
