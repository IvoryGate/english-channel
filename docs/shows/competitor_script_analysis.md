# Competitor Script Analysis — Series A & B Reference

Derived from deep reads of 10 competitor transcripts (5 per channel). Read-only analysis; no transcripts copied.

## Series A reference — Speak English With Class (English Leap Podcast)

**Hosts**: Anna (female, lead) + Jake (male, second). **Level**: B1–B2. **Views**: 100K–700K per episode.

### A.1 Cold-open chassis (frozen across all 5 episodes)

```
[brand sting] English Leap Podcast from Speak English with Class.
Hey, hey, English learners. Welcome back to the English Leap Podcast, your cozy
place to learn easy English through real life conversations and work on your
life at the same time.
I'm Anna.
And I am Jake. [+ hook-fusing line OR thesis line]
```

- Anna intros **first**, Jake fused on same/next line.
- Dual intro lives **inside the welcome sentence** (word ~20–60), never a separate beat.
- "Speak English With Class" spoken **only in cold-open sting**, never mid-episode.
- Show name ("English Leap Podcast") spoken at open + close only.

### A.2 Hook templates (5 patterns observed)

| ID | Pattern | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Slice-of-life small-talk → universal feeling | "Sunday-night dread" | Warm, low-stakes |
| B | Confessional mini-story → playful reaction | "I almost cancelled today's recording" | Character-driven comedy |
| C | Contrarian / paradox thesis | "a kind of strength that does not need to be loud" | Philosophical mindset |
| D | Parallel teaser + comic anecdote | "change your life if right / ruin your day if wrong" + "shouted at my coffee machine" | Behavior topic |
| E | Interactive game / question hook | "rate your life 1–10, but not seven" | Year-review / comeback |

### A.3 Dialogue rhythm

1. **Strict alternation** (Anna→Jake→Anna→Jake). Multi-line runs rare (≤4 sentences, only for brief stories).
2. **Short reactive beats as handoffs**: `Yeah.` / `Right?` / `Exactly.` / `Mhm.` / `Oh, I love that.` — connective tissue, not content.
3. **Medium teaching turns (2–3 sentences)** carry substance. Each ends with a handoff cue (question / metaphor / "Right?").
4. **Inline vocab glosses** — when a strong word first appears, the *other* host asks for meaning:
   ```
   >> I was relentless.
   >> Relentless. That means she didn't stop, right?
   >> Yes, she kept going again and again.
   ```
   Happens ~10× per episode. Signature teaching move.
5. **Filler discipline**: `uh`/`um`/`like`/`you know`/`I mean` essentially absent (pre-written scripts). Only `Mhm`/`M`/`Hm` as active-listening backchannels (~6–12 per episode).
6. **Interjections**: `Oh` (frequent), `Oof`/`Ouch` (empathy), `Wow` (surprise), `Ooh` (anticipation), `Aw` (warmth), `[laughter]` (~6–10× tagged).

### A.4 起承转合 master template

| Phase | Word position (of ~4000–4500) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 起 | 0–~15% | Cold-open sting → welcome + show name → dual intro → hook → topic announcement → B1/B2 callout → CTA + comment prompt → "who is this episode for" |
| 承 | ~15–80% | 3–5 teaching ideas, each: metaphor + personal anecdote + inline vocab gloss. Strict alternation. |
| 转 | ~80–90% | **Always Word Tour** — slow vocab recap of ~10 words. Format: `Word. Meaning. Two examples. "Repeat: ___."` |
| 合 | ~90–100% | Recap of 3–5 ideas in one breath → optional homework → comment prompt → CTA → sign-off sting: `This is Anna. / And this is Jake. / And you've been listening to the English Leap podcast. Bye.` |

### A.5 Series A differentiation

Class's moat is the **chassis**, not the topic. Series A (Ethan/Nora) borrows the chassis wholesale but differentiates on:
- **More natural filler tolerance** — allow `uh`/`um`/`you know` sparingly (Class forbids them; we permit 1 per 8–12 turns for human feel).
- **More emotional specificity** — per-turn `deliveryCue` (audiobook parity) instead of flat tone.
- **Ethan/Nora character dynamics** — Ethan = curious learner (asks the "dumb" question), Nora = warm peer coach (not teacher-student, but peer-who-knows-slightly-more).

## Series B reference — J and May Podcast

**Hosts**: May (female, teacher) + Jay (male, learner). **Level**: A2–B1. **Views**: 45K–644K per episode.

### B.1 Cold-open chassis (frozen across all 4 two-host episodes)

```
[brand sting] J and May Podcast.
Hi, I'm May.
And I'm Jay. [+ hook teaser line]
```

- May intros **first** (teacher), Jay second (learner).
- Intro is **fused with hook teaser** — no separate "welcome" sentence.
- Show name spoken in cold-open sting + close.

### B.2 Hook templates (3 patterns observed)

| ID | Pattern | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contrarian-number | "Only 5 Tenses (Not 12!)" — 644K views, channel's most viral | Rule-breaking / simplification topics |
| 2 | Mundane-scene | "You're at the coffee shop, the barista asks your name…" | Daily-situation topics |
| 3 | Embarrassment-story | "I once said 'I'm fine' at a funeral…" | Social-anxiety topics |

### B.3 Dialogue rhythm

1. **Strict May↔Jay alternation** with **1:3 turn-length ratio** — Jay (learner) takes short turns (1 sentence), May (teacher) takes longer turns (2–4 sentences).
2. **Learner-asks / teacher-explains / learner-mirrors / teacher-praises** pattern:
   ```
   Jay:  So wait, "used to" is only for the past?
   May:  Right. "Used to" locks the habit in the past. It means: not anymore.
   Jay:  Not anymore. Okay. So "I used to study French" means I don't now.
   May:  Exactly. You got it.
   ```
3. **Sparse fillers** — ≤2 `um`/`uh`/`hmm` per episode, **learner-side only** (Jay hesitates; May never fills).
4. **Jay carries emotion interjections**: `Oh!`, `Wait.`, `Really?`, `Hmm.` — May stays steady.
5. **Inline vocab glosses** — May glosses every new word immediately: `"Relentless — that means she didn't stop."`

### B.4 起承转合 master template

| Phase | Word position (of ~1400–1900) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 起 | 0–~25% | Brand sting → dual intro → contrarian-number hook → "By the end you will [X]" contract |
| 承 | ~25–70% | 3 teaching beats, each: learner question → teacher explanation → learner mirror → teacher praise. Short sentences (8–12 words). Gloss every new word. |
| 转 | ~70–85% | **Meta pivot** — mindset / identity / role-play (not just content). "But here's the thing most learners miss…" |
| 合 | ~85–100% | One-sentence recap → **one-sentence comment challenge** → CTA → sign-off |

### B.5 Series B differentiation

J&May's moat is the **contrarian-number hook + learner-asks rhythm**. Series B (Riley/Sam) borrows both but differentiates on:
- **Sam gets more personality** — J&May's Jay is a flat learner-proxy; our Sam has doubts, jokes, mild resistance (more like a real friend).
- **More filler tolerance on Sam's side** — `uh`, `emmm`, `hmm` allowed on learner turns (Jay is too clean; real A2 learners hesitate).
- **Per-turn `deliveryCue`** — audiobook parity for emotional specificity.
- **Brand name "English Listening Room"** spoken once in opening (J&May doesn't say their show name mid-episode; we will, for brand-building).

## Cross-channel insights for all three ELR series

1. **Frozen cold-open chassis is non-negotiable** — both channels use an identical 4–6 line opening every episode. Consistency = brand recognition = returning viewers.
2. **Inline vocab gloss is the signature teaching move** — the *other* host asks "what does X mean?" ~10× per episode. This is what makes it "learning" not "chat".
3. **Filler discipline differs by level** — Class (B1-B2) forbids true fillers; J&May (A2-B1) allows sparse learner-side fillers. ELR should match: A sparse, B moderate, C near-zero.
4. **转 is always a structural pivot, not just content** — Word Tour (Class) or meta-mindset (J&May). Never just "more teaching".
5. **合 always has a comment challenge** — one concrete thing for viewers to write. Drives engagement.
6. **Show name spoken at open + close** — brand reinforcement. ELR must say "English Listening Room" at least once per episode (current drafts say it zero times).
