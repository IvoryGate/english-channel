# ELR Human Long-Form Script Method

This method converts the August 2026 competitor and TED transcript study into a repeatable writing system. It is a structural method, not a source-text library: competitor and TED wording must never be copied into an ELR script.

## What the study actually found

Competitor scripts are not uniformly better than ours. Many contain the same repeated affirmations, padded definitions, and artificial host symmetry that we want to remove. Their strongest episodes do four things better: they open inside a specific human moment, let a story run long enough to matter, organize the episode around one memorable device, and return to the opening tension at the end.

The TED transcripts add five useful principles:

- make the audience care before explaining;
- organize the talk around one well-formed idea;
- let the speaker be implicated in the problem instead of sounding omniscient;
- turn explanation into a demonstration or changed-condition example;
- land on a consequence or callback instead of reading back a summary.

These principles agree with the official [TEDx Speaker Guide](https://storage.ted.com/tedx/manuals/tedxspeakerguide.pdf): establish why the idea matters, explain it with evidence, keep the structure invisible, cut nonessential material, address reasonable counterarguments, and rehearse until concise writing sounds spontaneous.

## The human spine

Every long episode follows one invisible dramatic spine:

1. **Human moment.** Put a named person in a recognizable place with a concrete objective.
2. **Open tension.** Something fails, remains misunderstood, or carries a social cost.
3. **One device.** Give the viewer one phrase, contrast, image, or decision rule to hold.
4. **Changed conditions.** Test it in at least two situations where pressure or meaning changes.
5. **Demonstration.** Let the hosts try, fail, interrupt, and repair in real time.
6. **Callback landing.** Return to the first moment and show what now changes.

Internal headings may help production, but hosts must not announce an outline or syllabus.

## Runtime designs

### Standard episode: 10–15 minutes

- 00:00–00:30: specific scene and open tension;
- 00:30–02:00: narrow promise and first usable line;
- 02:00–07:00: two changed-condition scenes;
- 07:00–11:00: guided practice or live repair;
- 11:00–14:00: opening-scene callback and one viewer action.

### Deep Practice: 25–35 minutes

- 00:00–00:45: inciting scene with an unanswered question;
- 00:45–02:00: one central idea and an immediate usable move;
- 02:00–07:00: first complete scene or story;
- 07:00–12:00: complication, disagreement, or failed attempt;
- 12:00–18:00: guided practice with decreasing support;
- 18:00–24:00: new context and pattern interruption;
- 24:00–30:00: natural-speed replay and callback;
- 30:00–35:00: optional final challenge and next-episode bridge.

Every two to four minutes, something must change: scene, pressure, question, speaker goal, listening speed, or participation mode.

## Anti-AI gates

- No generic welcome, CEFR label, or show description before the stake.
- No visible agenda language such as “first we will” or “in today’s lesson”.
- Each host owns a constraint, memory, or opinion; neither is a permanent answer machine.
- Remove empty turns that only say `Exactly`, `Perfect`, `Nice`, `Great`, `Right`, or `Absolutely`.
- Do not write a learner question whose only purpose is to trigger a prepared lecture.
- Avoid equal-length ping-pong across an entire section.
- Use at most one central metaphor system; callbacks are more valuable than new metaphors.
- Leave some inference to the viewer. Do not explain every nuance.
- A teaching phrase may repeat, but the context or consequence must change by the fourth use.
- The ending changes the viewer's lens or next action; it does not summarize a list.

## Pre-render editorial card

Before synthesis, record:

- the opening scene and social/practical stake;
- the one communicative outcome;
- the central device;
- the first usable line and timestamp target;
- the host disagreement or failed attempt;
- the changed conditions;
- the opening callback;
- the previous same-series episode and the structural difference.

The script validator flags empty-confirmation density as an editorial warning. A warning does not invalidate a legacy packaged episode, but every newly written script must resolve it before rendering.
