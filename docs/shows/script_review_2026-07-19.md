## Series A — Episode 001 (Ethan and Nora, B1-B2)

Title: "English Podcast For Easy English Conversation | Talk About Yourself Without Sounding Arrogant | Learn English"
Topic: self-introductions that sound confident, not boastful
Hook template used: B (confessional mini-story)

### 1. Human feel — 7/10

The hosts mostly sound like real people. Ethan's confessional opener is specific and funny, and the long/short sentence variation is well handled. Fillers are sparse (one "Um..." at line 121, one "Hmm." at line 92), which matches the Series A "sparse, on Ethan" rule. Multi-line runs are present but conservative.

Examples that work:

- "I said, 'I work in customer support.' And then I took a sip of water. A long sip. Like the water was the most interesting thing in the room." (Ethan, line 64) — vivid, self-deprecating, real.
- "You know what I used to do? I used to rehearse my intro in the bathroom mirror. Word for word. And then at the actual dinner, I would forget every word because someone coughed." (Ethan, line 210) — specific image, real feeling.
- "The truth bends. I like that." (Ethan, line 220) — short reactive beat, natural handoff.

Examples that feel stiff:

- "Plain English — what does that mean here? Like, simple words?" (Ethan, line 92) — the "Like, simple words?" tag is a setup line that no real B1-B2 learner would phrase so cleanly; it reads as a cue for the teacher.
- "Without performing. That means not acting, not pretending, not saying what you think they want to hear. Just being real." (Nora, line 254) — the Word Tour definition is a list, not a voice.

### 2. Hook — 8/10

Confessional mini-story (Template B) executed cleanly. The freeze-at-work-dinner image is concrete and the "long sip of water" beat lands the embarrassment before Nora reacts.

Hook quote: "Nora, I have to tell you something. I went to a work dinner last week, and someone said, 'So, tell me about yourself.' And I froze. I gave them my job title and then... nothing." (Ethan, line 56)

Judgment: correct template for Series A, stakes are personal, and the confession doubles as the episode's premise. Slight ding: the hook is good but not surprising — "I froze when asked about myself" is a well-worn confession. Could push for one more specific oddity (e.g., what the person's face looked like) to lift it from relatable to memorable.

### 3. Self-intro + brand name — 5/10

The intro order is muddled. The style guide rule (A.1) says "Nora intros first (coach), Ethan second (learner)." The script does:

- Nora: "Hey, hey, English learners. Welcome back to Daily Talk..." (welcome line)
- Ethan: "I'm Ethan."
- Nora: "And I'm Nora. And this is the English Listening Room."

So Nora opens the welcome, but the "I'm X" order is Ethan first, then Nora. The "I'm Ethan" / "And I'm Nora" pair inverts the rule. It also breaks the frozen chassis example in the style guide, which shows Nora saying "I'm Nora" first.

Brand name: "English Listening Room" spoken once by Nora. Correct.

Fusion with hook: the welcome + dual intro + brand + confession all flow in one block. Good fusion.

Verdict on this dimension: brand and fusion are fine, but the intro-order violation is a P1 fix because the chassis is supposed to be frozen.

### 4. 起承转合 proportion (target 30/40/20/10) — 7/10

Estimated word positions (script ~1900 words):

- 起 (Intro Hook): lines 1-80 ≈ 30% — on target.
- 承 (Teaching + Micro-Pocket + Recycle): lines 82-220 ≈ 50% — over the 40% target. The Recycle section (lines 184-220) is doing extra work that arguably belongs in 转.
- 转 (Word Tour): lines 222-290 ≈ 20% — on target.
- 合 (Recap + CTA): lines 292-310 ≈ 10% — on target.

Judgment: 起/转/合 are clean. 承 is ~10 points heavy because the Recycle beat is folded into 承 instead of being treated as the transition into 转. Either trim the Recycle or reclassify it as the start of 转.

### 5. deliveryCue quality — 7/10

Most cues are natural-language and verb-forward, but a noticeable share are single-word or flat.

Good cues (3):

- "confessional mini-story, self-conscious" (line 56)
- "learner pushback, half-joking but real" (line 184)
- "facilitator, landing the point" (line 208)

Weak cues (2):

- "warm" (line 167) — flat label, no shape.
- "amused" (line 108) — single-word emotion, not a performance phrase.
- "steady" (line 218) — same; could be "steady, letting the line land."

### 6. Differentiation from competitors (vs Class) — 6/10

The chassis is borrowed wholesale from Class (the style guide is honest about this). The confessional hook is Class's Template B. Differentiation relies on (a) filler tolerance on Ethan, (b) per-turn deliveryCue, (c) peer-not-teacher dynamic, (d) brand name spoken.

What works: Ethan's "Um..." and "Hmm." are present; deliveryCues are on every turn; Nora does feel like a peer, not a teacher ("That is so relatable. And it is exactly what we are fixing today."); brand name is spoken.

What feels close to Class: the Word Tour format ("Word. Meaning. Example. Repeat.") is a direct lift, and the inline vocab gloss pattern ("Plain English — what does that mean here?") is the same signature move. The peer dynamic is the strongest differentiator and it does land, but the bones are still Class's bones.

### 7. Teaching effectiveness — 8/10

A B1-B2 learner walks away with a usable three-beat formula (what I do / what I care about / one small win), the "plain English vs jargon" distinction with concrete before/after pairs ("I handle inbound escalations" → "I answer the hard emails"), the "friend test" for self-checking, and the mindset line "the truth bends." The inline glosses hit ~8-10 times (plain English, glamorous, jargon, boastful, flat, performing). This is solid B1-B2 teaching — the framework is memorable and the examples are concrete.

### Series A overall verdict: PASS WITH REVISIONS

The script is publishable but has one P1 (intro order) and several P2s (Recycle placement, flat cues, hook specificity).

Top 5 revision suggestions (Series A):

1. Fix the intro order. Current: "Ethan: I'm Ethan. / Nora: And I'm Nora." (lines 50-54). Swap to "Nora: I'm Nora. / Ethan: And I'm Ethan." to match A.1's "Nora first (coach), Ethan second (learner)" rule. Why: the chassis is supposed to be frozen; this is brand consistency.
2. Sharpen the hook with one oddity. Current: "I gave them my job title and then... nothing." (line 56). Add one specific image — e.g., "I gave them my job title and then I stared at the bread basket like it owed me an answer." Why: relatable is good; memorable is better, and Class's confessions usually have one odd image.
3. Reclassify or trim the Recycle. Lines 184-220 are ~15% of the script and push 承 to ~50%. Either move the Recycle into 转 (so 转 becomes "Recycle + Word Tour" at ~30%) or cut one beat (the "truth bends" exchange, lines 214-220, could be folded into the Word Tour). Why: the 30/40/20/10 ratio is the series signature.
4. Replace flat cues. "warm" (line 167), "amused" (line 108), "steady" (line 218) — rewrite as "warm, half-smiling", "amused, leaning back", "steady, letting the line land". Why: the style guide explicitly says cues are natural-language performance phrases, not enums.
5. Soften the teacher-setup line. "Plain English — what does that mean here? Like, simple words?" (line 92) — drop the "Like, simple words?" tag or make it messier: "Plain English — wait, what does that mean, just... normal words?" Why: the current line reads as a scripted cue for Nora; a real learner would not phrase the question so neatly.

## Series B — Episode 002 (Riley and Sam, A2-B1)

Title: "You Only Need 5 Tenses to Speak Confidently (Not 12!) | Easy English Podcast A2-B1"
Topic: minimum viable tense set for speaking
Hook template used: 1 (contrarian-number)

### 1. Human feel — 7/10

Sam has real personality — doubts, jokes, self-deprecation — which is exactly the Series B differentiation from J&May's flat Jay. Fillers land on Sam ("Emmm..." at lines 38, 88, 218; "Hmm..." at lines 56, 138) at roughly the right density. Riley stays clean. Long/short variation is good; Sam's turns are short, Riley's are medium.

Examples that work:

- "Emmm... I learned twelve tenses in school. I still freeze when I speak. So maybe five is good." (Sam, line 38) — natural hesitation, real opinion.
- "I am the worst freezer I know." (Sam, line 240) — self-deprecating, personality.
- "Next to my toothbrush. So I read it twice a day." (Sam, line 264) — funny, specific image.

Examples that feel stiff:

- "Five lines. Five tenses. One minute. You just practiced more than most learners do in a week." (Riley, line 226) — slogan cadence, sounds like marketing copy.
- "Present simple is for habits. Things you do every day, every week, always. 'I work.' 'I eat.' 'I sleep.' All habits." (Riley, line 94) — list-like teaching beat; the "I work / I eat / I sleep" triplet is mechanical.

### 2. Hook — 5/10

The contrarian-number template is executed correctly, but the angle is a near-direct copy of J&May's most viral episode ("Only 5 Tenses (Not 12!)" — 644K views, cited in the competitor analysis). The style guide's C.7-equivalent (and the competitor analysis) explicitly forbids "Stealing specific hooks, numbered list angles, or anecdotes from competitor samples."

Hook quote: "Today. Only five tenses. Not twelve. Five." / "Sounds wrong, right? But by the end, you will use these five like a native speaker in daily talk." (Sam + Riley, lines 34-36)

Judgment: template-correct, but the specific "5 tenses not 12" angle is J&May's signature. This is the single biggest issue in the script. Either change the number/topic (e.g., "Only 3 sentence patterns. Not 12." or "Only 4 verbs you really need. Not 50.") or pick a different hook template (mundane-scene or embarrassment-story) for this episode.

### 3. Self-intro + brand name — 9/10

Intro order matches the chassis exactly:

- Riley: "Hi, I'm Riley."
- Sam: "And I'm Sam. Welcome to the English Listening Room." (lines 28-30)

Riley first (coach), Sam second (learner). Brand name spoken once by Sam. Hook teaser follows immediately. Clean.

Minor ding: the chassis in the style guide puts the hook teaser on Sam's line ("And I'm Sam. Welcome to the English Listening Room. [+ hook teaser line]"), but the script puts the hook on a separate Sam turn ("Today. Only five tenses. Not twelve. Five." at line 34). This is fine — the fusion is still tight — but it is a small deviation from "fused on the same line."

### 4. 起承转合 proportion (target 25/45/20/10) — 7/10

Estimated word positions (script ~1500 words):

- 起 (Intro Hook + contract): lines 1-42 ≈ 25% — on target.
- 承 (Parts 1-3, teaching + mindset): lines 44-200 ≈ 50% — slightly over the 45% target.
- 转 (Meta Pivot): lines 202-244 ≈ 20% — on target.
- 合 (Recap + CTA): lines 246-280 ≈ 10% — on target.

Judgment: close to spec. The 承 is ~5 points heavy because Part 3 ("the mindset shift," lines 172-200) overlaps with the 转 ("Meta Pivot," lines 202-244) thematically — both are about mindset. Consider folding Part 3's "wrong tense is better than no sentence" beat into the Meta Pivot so 转 carries the full mindset shift.

### 5. deliveryCue quality — 7/10

Good cues (3):

- "hesitant learner, searching" (line 48)
- "patient clear explanation" (line 52)
- "relieved understanding" (line 154)

Weak cues (2):

- "warm" (line 102) — flat.
- "affirm" (line 96) — verb but no shape; could be "affirm, slight nod".
- "warm laugh" (line 162) — combined laugh + warmth but still generic.

### 6. Differentiation from competitors (vs J&May) — 4/10

This is the script's weakest dimension. The hook is J&May's signature angle (5 tenses not 12). The learner-asks / teacher-explains / learner-mirrors / teacher-praises 4-beat loop is also J&May's signature rhythm, executed almost identically. Sam's personality ("I am the worst freezer I know," "Next to my toothbrush") is the one clear differentiator and it does land.

What works: Sam has doubts and jokes (Jay doesn't); fillers on Sam are present (Jay's are too clean); per-turn deliveryCue is on every turn; brand name is spoken.

What feels like a copy: the hook angle, the 4-beat loop, the "five tenses" topic itself. A listener who knows J&May would clock this as a cover. The style guide's B.5 differentiators are present in mechanics but not in topic. Fix the hook and the topic, and the rest of the differentiation holds.

### 7. Teaching effectiveness — 8/10

An A2-B1 learner walks away with: the five tenses (present simple, present continuous, past simple, present perfect, going to), the "minimum viable" concept, the "wrong tense is better than no sentence" mindset, and a concrete "five lines a day about your real day" practice routine. The practice lines (water example, lines 222-224) are immediately usable. Strong A2-B1 teaching — the framework is small enough to actually do.

Minor gap: the style guide says "Gloss ~6-8 words per episode." The script glosses "minimum viable" and the tense names, but there are very few non-grammar vocab glosses (no "relentless"-style word). Adding 1-2 non-grammar glosses would help.

### Series B overall verdict: MAJOR REVISIONS NEEDED

The hook is too close to J&May's signature episode. Everything else is publishable, but the hook problem is a P0 brand-risk issue.

Top 5 revision suggestions (Series B):

1. Replace the hook angle. Current: "Today. Only five tenses. Not twelve. Five." (line 34). Change to a different contrarian-number (e.g., "Today. Only three sentence patterns. Not twelve grammar rules. Three.") or switch to Template 2 (mundane-scene: "You are at the coffee shop. Someone asks about your weekend. You freeze.") or Template 3 (embarrassment-story). Why: "5 tenses not 12" is J&May's most viral hook and the style guide forbids stealing numbered list angles.
2. Add 1-2 non-grammar vocab glosses. Current glosses are all tense names. Add a word like "freeze" (line 38) — Riley: "Freeze — that means you stop, you cannot speak, like your body is stuck." Why: the style guide calls for ~6-8 vocab glosses and the current count is thin on non-grammar words.
3. Fold Part 3 into the Meta Pivot. Lines 172-200 (Part 3 mindset) and lines 202-244 (Meta Pivot) both cover mindset. Merge so 转 carries the full mindset shift and 承 stays at ~45%. Why: the 25/45/20/10 ratio is the series signature and 承 is currently ~50%.
4. Replace flat cues. "warm" (line 102), "affirm" (line 96), "warm laugh" (line 162) — rewrite as "warm, slight smile", "affirm, nodding", "warm laugh, surprised". Why: cues should be performance phrases, not enums.
5. Soften the slogan beat. "Five lines. Five tenses. One minute. You just practiced more than most learners do in a week." (Riley, line 226) — break the cadence: "Five lines. Five tenses. One minute. That is more practice than most learners get in a week, honestly." Why: the current rhythm sounds like marketing copy and breaks Riley's "patient clear coach" character.

## Series C — Episode 001 (Leo and Mia, B2-C1)

Title: "Give Feedback in English Without Sounding Cold | Polished English Podcast B2-C1"
Topic: observation-impact-request feedback frame
Hook template used: T1 (thesis-first paradox)

### 1. Human feel — 8/10

This is the strongest of the three scripts on human feel. Mia's voice is specific and self-aware, and the pushback beats are real, not token. The long/short variation is well handled. The one weakness is that fillers are near-zero — the style guide allows Mia 1 per 6-8 turns, but I counted zero explicit "uh"/"um"/"hmm" in Mia's turns. The script leans on punctuation and wording for hesitation instead, which is good practice per C.3, but a few real fillers would help.

Examples that work:

- "I sent feedback last week and reread it with actual horror. I meant helpful. It landed like a cold email from a robot manager." (Mia, line 62) — vivid, specific, real voice.
- "I would write the feedback email, then rewrite it, then delete it, then write a completely different one, then close my laptop and send nothing. Three hours of work for zero feedback." (Mia, line 224) — specific cascade, funny, real.
- "Three sentences. I have been writing novels in my head for years. And the reader never saw any of them. They just got the muddy paragraph at the end." (Mia, line 236) — payoff line, lands the metaphor.

Examples that feel stiff:

- "The frame is not a guarantee. It is a probability. It raises the chance they hear you. It does not promise they will." (Leo, line 168) — aphorism stack, sounds written.
- "Neither one alone is enough." (Leo, line 152) — textbook cadence in the middle of a teaching beat.

### 2. Hook — 9/10

Thesis-first paradox (T1) executed cleanly and woven with a story beat inside the first 30 seconds. The paradox is genuinely non-obvious and matches the B2-C1 register.

Hook quote: "Soft English can still feel sharp. And the kinder you try to sound, the less clear you sometimes become. That is the trap we are fixing today." (Leo, line 58)

Judgment: correct template for Series C, intellectual without being dry, and Mia's "Oh, that hits" reaction grounds it immediately. The hook also sets up the whole episode's tension (kind vs clear). This is the strongest hook of the three scripts.

### 3. Self-intro + brand name — 8/10

Intro order matches the chassis:

- Leo: "Hey. Welcome back to Polished English, the show where two people talk about real life, and you overhear the English you actually need."
- Mia: "I'm Mia."
- Leo: "And I'm Leo. And this is the English Listening Room." (lines 48-54)

Mia says "I'm Mia" first (listener voice), Leo says "I'm Leo" second (facilitator). Matches the style guide rule "Mia first, Leo second." Brand name spoken once by Leo. Hook fused on the same beat.

Minor ding: Leo opens the welcome line, which is correct per the chassis, but the "Mia first" rule is about the "I'm X" order, not the welcome. The script handles this correctly. The only nit: the welcome line is long (32 words) before Mia gets to speak; trimming it slightly would let the "I'm Mia" land faster.

### 4. 起承转合 proportion (target 20/40/25/15) — 9/10

Estimated word positions (script ~2200 words):

- 起 (Intro + hook + contract + level callout): lines 1-72 ≈ 20% — on target.
- 承 (Three threads + micro-pocket): lines 74-188 ≈ 40% — on target.
- 转 (Pattern interrupt + conflict recycle): lines 190-240 ≈ 25% — on target.
- 合 (Word tour + close): lines 242-300 ≈ 15% — on target.

Judgment: this is the best-structured of the three scripts. The 20/40/25/15 ratio is clean, the micro-pocket sits after the first thread (correct), and the 转 carries a real pattern interrupt + Mia resistance + conflict recycle (not just "more teaching"). The Word Tour is correctly placed inside 合 as the payoff, with the honest stay-payoff line before it.

### 5. deliveryCue quality — 8/10

Good cues (3):

- "listener pushback, half-joking but real" (line 200)
- "quiet conviction" (line 90)
- "facilitator, leaning in" (line 66)

Weak cues (2):

- "warm" (line 116) — flat.
- "thoughtful" (line 104) — single-word emotion.
- "facilitator, warm" (line 178) — two flat labels stacked, no shape.

### 6. Differentiation from competitors (vs Class and J&May) — 9/10

This script sounds least like a competitor. The thesis-first hook is distinct from Class's scene/confession and J&May's contrarian-number. The daily-talk fiction register ("sounds like two people discussing a topic, not a class") is achieved — there is no "today we teach you," no syllabus voice. Mia's pushback is structural (转), not token, which neither competitor does. The "novel in your head" metaphor and the "three sentences, not a novel" payoff are original framings. Per-turn deliveryCue and brand name are present.

What works: register is genuinely conversational, pushback is real, the frame (observation-impact-request) is taught through dialogue not lecture, the honest payoff line ("if you stayed this long") is anti-bait-and-switch.

What could be stronger: a few more Mia fillers would push the human feel further from the "pre-written script" register that both competitors share.

### 7. Teaching effectiveness — 9/10

A B2-C1 learner walks away with: the three-move frame (observation, impact, request), the "always vs three times" distinction (verdict vs fact), the "hedge" concept with concrete words ("just," "maybe," "I think"), the "put the reason in the sentence" rule, and the "split the frame across days" permission for when they freeze. The inline polish beats ("circle back" style) are present but lighter than the style guide's 6-8 target — I counted ~4 polish moments (hedge, observation, impact, request). The teaching is concrete, immediately usable, and pitched exactly at B2-C1. The "novel in your head" metaphor makes the lesson sticky.

And honestly, that is hard to hear." Why: four aphorisms in a row sounds written; a reaction beat re-humanizes it.
4. Add 1-2 more inline polish beats. Current polish moments are ~4 (hedge, observation, impact, request). Add one for "circumlocution" or "soft but clear" from the key phrases — e.g. Leo tightening Mia's "I guess maybe we could try" to "Could we try". Why: the style guide calls for 6-8 polish beats and the key phrases list includes words that do not get polished in the script.
5. Trim the welcome line slightly. "Hey. Welcome back to Polished English, the show where two people talk about real life, and you overhear the English you actually need." (Leo, line 48) — 32 words before Mia speaks. Trim to ~20: "Hey. Welcome back to Polished English, where two people talk about real life and you overhear the English you actually need." Why: a shorter welcome lets the "I'm Mia" land faster and tightens the 起.

6. **Teaching effectiveness is strong across all three.** Each script delivers a memorable framework (three-beat intro / five tenses / observation-impact-request) with concrete examples at the right CEFR level. The signature inline gloss / polish move is present in all three, though Series B is thin on non-grammar glosses and Series C is slightly under the 6-8 polish beat target.
