# ELR Revised Pilot Briefs

These are deliberately limited pilots, not a mass rewrite of the existing catalogue. They test the new situation-first standard before production is scaled.

## Series A — Daily Talk (B1–B2)

- **Title:** `Someone Says “Tell Me About Yourself” — What Do You Say? | B1 English`
- **Description opening:** `Someone asks about you at a work dinner and your mind goes blank after your job title. Ethan and Nora rehearse one warm three-beat answer you can adapt without sounding rehearsed or arrogant.`
- **Engine:** Failed moment → repair.
- **Cold open:** Ethan has already answered with only his job title; Nora challenges the unhelpful line before either host introduces the show.
- **First usable line:** `I work in customer support, and lately I have been making our help articles easier to use.`
- **Test:** Can a listener make a 20-second self-introduction with role + one human detail + a return question?

## Series B — First Steps (A2–B1)

- **Title:** `I Can’t Hear You on the Phone — Say This in English | A2–B1`
- **Description opening:** `The call starts, you miss the first sentence, and panic makes you say “yes” anyway. Riley and Sam practise three short repair lines so you can ask for slower, clearer English without freezing.`
- **Engine:** Live rehearsal.
- **Cold open:** A phone call begins; Sam misunderstands the first request and has to recover.
- **First usable line:** `Sorry, could you say that again, please?`
- **Test:** Can a listener complete a three-turn clarification exchange by the 90-second mark?

## Series C — Polished English (B2–C1)

- **Title:** `Your English Feedback Sounds Cold? Use This 3-Sentence Work Script | B2–C1`
- **Description opening:** `You rewrite a feedback message until it is either blunt or meaningless. Mia and Leo turn one tense client-call moment into an observation, an impact, and a clear request you can use at work.`
- **Engine:** Two plausible choices.
- **Cold open:** Mia reads two bad versions of the same message; Leo refuses to call either one “professional.”
- **First usable line:** `I noticed the draft arrived after the client call, which meant we could not answer their pricing question live.`
- **Test:** Can a listener replace one vague or accusatory sentence with a concrete three-part feedback line?
