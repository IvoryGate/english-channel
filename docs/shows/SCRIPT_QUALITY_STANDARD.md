# ELR Script Quality Standard

Use this standard for every new or substantially revised A/B/C episode. It supplements the series bibles and supersedes any instruction to use a cold open or teaching loop verbatim.

For 18–45 minute episodes, also follow `docs/shows/LONG_FORM_SCRIPT_METHOD.md`. Its human spine, runtime gates, and anti-AI gates are required, not optional inspiration.

## The viewer contract

Each episode must answer all four questions within its first 20 seconds:

1. **Whose moment is this?** Name or dramatize one recognisable situation.
2. **What goes wrong?** Show the social, emotional, or practical stake.
3. **What English will become easier?** Promise one narrow, observable result.
4. **Why these two hosts?** Give them different instincts, not coach-and-proxy roles.

The show name may appear once between 30 and 75 seconds. Do not spend the cold open on greetings, CEFR labels, a Word Tour promise, a subscribe request, or a comment question.

## Script architecture rotation

Do not repeat the same architecture in adjacent episodes of a series. Choose one primary engine and record it in the draft header.

| Engine | Opening move | Middle | Learning payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Failed moment → repair | A host has just mishandled a real situation | Reconstruct the exchange and try two repairs | A listener can rehearse the replacement line |
| Live rehearsal | Begin with the line the viewer needs | One host resists or misuses it | The line returns under a changed condition |
| Two plausible choices | Hosts disagree about what to say | Test each choice against a consequence | Explain the trade-off, not a rule list |
| Story with interruption | A short personal story creates a problem | The other host interrupts at the decisive wording | Reuse the phrase in a new scene |
| Listener case file | Start from a specific listener message | Diagnose the exact sentence, then role-play it | Give one next action for this week |

Three teaching points are not a default architecture. Use a single communicative task, or at most two linked moves, unless the topic genuinely needs more.

## Human dialogue rules

- Each host needs an independent point of view, memory, or constraint. A learner host must sometimes disagree, change the subject, make a reasonable mistake, or reject the proposed wording.
- Replace praise loops (`Exactly`, `You got it`, `Nice`) with a response that moves the situation forward. One short affirmation is fine; repeated affirmation is not dialogue.
- Put the first usable English line or mini-role-play within 90 seconds, then return to it later under new pressure.
- A phrase may be deliberately repeated for rehearsal, but never more than three times in the same wording without a new context or consequence.
- Explanations must emerge from what a host just said. Do not have a host ask an obviously prewritten question only to trigger a lecture.
- Keep a small amount of interruption, self-correction, and unequal turn length where natural. Do not manufacture filler words to simulate humanity.

## Accurate promises

Do not promise that one small framework lets a learner say "almost anything", sound native, or solve a broad confidence problem. Promise one checkable outcome such as:

- finish a 20-second self-introduction;
- ask for clarification on a phone call;
- give feedback using observation, impact, and a request;
- join a conversation already in progress with one bridge line.

Life, psychology, and philosophy topics must make similarly narrow promises.
They may help a viewer examine a thought, compare two choices, or try one
reflection practice. They must not promise to cure anxiety, depression,
trauma, insomnia, or another health condition. Attribute historical ideas and
quotes only after checking a reliable source; do not write a motivational
speech in the voice of a living public figure.

## Titles and descriptions

### Title

- Lead with the situation or tension; the first 45 characters must make sense without the channel name.
- Put `English`, the CEFR level, or the format only in the suffix when they clarify audience fit.
- Use no generic prefix such as `English Podcast For Daily Life`.
- Target 45–75 characters; the hard upload limit remains 100 characters.
- Avoid repeated `Not X` formulas in consecutive Series B episodes.

### Description

The first two sentences must state: (1) the viewer's moment and (2) the usable spoken result. Then add level, hosts, chapter/timestamp material, one engagement question, and a light subscribe CTA. Avoid a keyword paragraph that merely repeats `English listening`, `podcast`, and `confidence`.

## Pre-render review

Before human approval, the writer must state:

- the selected architecture;
- the first usable line and where it first appears;
- the one checkable learner outcome;
- the title's situation-first hook;
- what differs from the prior episode in the same series.

The validator's empty-confirmation warning must be zero for every newly written script. Legacy packaged episodes may retain a warning only when changing the draft would invalidate already rendered audio/video; record that exception in the weekly plan.

For `flagship_40`, the review card must also include projected runtime, spoken
word count, a stimulus-change map, source notes, and the probed rendered-media
duration. A label, title, or manifest target does not override the measured
duration gate.
