## Show description (YouTube playlist / show)

Welcome to **Polished English**, the B2-C1 advanced English podcast from English Listening Room — the show where fluent listeners sharpen the one thing fluency alone can't give you: *clarity*.

Leo and Mia don't teach grammar. They talk — about the moments where "good English" still isn't enough. Why you sound polite but stay unclear at work. Why the room felt wrong even after everyone agreed. Why fluent people still avoid the hard sentence. Each episode takes one concrete situation and follows it through a real turn: a hook, a development, a pivot, and a landing — the way an actual good conversation moves, not the way a vocabulary list does.

**Who it's for.** If you're B2-C1 — comfortable, fluent, even confident — but you've started noticing the gap between *sounding right* and *landing right*, this is your show. We use longer sentences (18-25 words), idioms, abstract nouns, conditionals, concession, and subtle register shifts. We assume you don't need glosses; we give you nuance, pushback, and the kind of second-reading that makes your next email, meeting, or hard conversation actually land.

**What you'll get every episode.**
- A thesis-first hook — one concrete situation and the paradox inside it
- A question-flow conversation with real pushback (Mia doesn't just agree; Leo doesn't just teach)
- A "micro-pocket" that slows the key phrasing, plus a conflict-recycle that revisits the tension
- An honest "Word Tour" of 2-4 pre-heard items — no inflated promises, just what the phrase really buys you
- An anti-listicle structure: at most 2-3 threads, developed, not stacked

**How to listen.** Episodes are standalone but worth re-listening — the nuance shifts on a second pass. New episodes every two weeks, under 20 minutes at natural speed. Bring a notebook for the Word Tour; the rest, just listen like you'd listen to a smart friend.

**Part of the English Listening Room ladder:** First Steps (A2-B1) → Daily Talk (B1-B2) → **Polished English (B2-C1)** → Classic Listening audiobooks. This is the top of the conversation ladder — come here when "fluent" stops feeling like enough.

Subscribe for B2-C1 advanced English that trades vocabulary lists for the thing you actually wanted: to be understood, precisely, when it matters.
