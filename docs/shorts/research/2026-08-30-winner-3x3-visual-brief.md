# Winner 3×3 Visual Research Brief — 2026-08-30

## Evidence

The immutable runtime capture is
`workspace/shorts/research/winner-3x3-2026-08-30.json`; its 30-image phone-size
contact sheet is stored beside it. The cohort was collected from four public
YouTube searches covering real-life conversation, polite/natural phrasing,
American expressions, and English-learning Shorts. Every row records the
source channel, video ID/URL, public view count, publication age estimate,
views per day, and original thumbnail URL.

The cohort is deliberately high-view rather than merely recent: examples range
from tens of thousands to more than 22 million public views. It includes direct
English-learning channels and adjacent podcast/conversation formats. These are
observations, not causal attribution.

## Observable Patterns

- A single face or two-person interaction dominates most compositions. Women
  are prominent in 23 of the 30 sampled thumbnails.
- Bright blue, yellow, coral, cream, red, and white fields outperform muddy
  low-contrast palettes in the visible cohort.
- The text promise is usually one idea: a number, a result, a mistake to avoid,
  or a real-life setting. It is large enough to read at contact-sheet size.
- Faces show a legible emotion or gesture: delight, surprise, uncertainty,
  pointing, invitation, or correction.
- Real-life props such as a kitchen, café, office, microphone, phone, or flag
  identify the use case before the title is read.
- One high-view example uses bright anime dialogue with two adults and a
  concrete service encounter, supporting illustration when the social action
  remains immediately clear.
- Generic podcast-room darkness, decorative empty space, and many small text
  claims are weak fits for mobile discovery even when aesthetically polished.

## Channel Hypotheses

The three channel winners share a stronger content pattern than the rest of
the visible cohort: dialogue, one socially useful phrase, a specific everyday
setting, and a small interpretation decision. Their current warm/dark artwork
did not prevent distribution, so content is the leading hypothesis; it is not
a reason to keep the dark palette.

For the nine iterations:

1. Keep one adult woman as the viewer-identification anchor and make the second
   person an active conversation partner.
2. Use bright anime illustration, high-key daylight or colorful practical
   lighting, and a distinct setting/action for every Short.
3. Reserve clean mobile caption space, but keep faces and the social object
   large enough to read without text.
4. Use the generated scene as the video background; apply the channel's tested
   cover system for the editorial headline and CEFR marker.
5. Reject repeated camera staging, dim faces, brown-on-brown grading, childlike
   school imagery, anatomical artifacts, or a scene that does not express the
   exact speech act.

## 3×3 Content Matrix

| Parent signal | Adjacent phrase transfer | Interactive context choice | Social repair/consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning decoder | `I'm all set` | two meanings of `I'm good` | tone behind `I'm fine` |
| Natural ordering | lunch request | `Can I get` vs `Give me` | politely correct a wrong drink |
| Polite refusal | `Maybe another time` | real yes vs soft no | decline while offering an alternative |

The matrix changes one primary content-form variable per child while retaining
dialogue, practical American English, female audience anchoring, and comparable
runtime. Time slot remains a recorded nuisance variable because the owner
requested all nine releases within two days.
